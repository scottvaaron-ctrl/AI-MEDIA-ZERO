### G-topic: One real place with a surprising true story
stage: topic
Pick one named, real place (a town, building, border, island, road or site) and the one surprising true event or fact that makes it worth 30 seconds. The surprise must be in the sources, not invented. Skip press-release news, product launches, listicles, and anything whose only visuals would be text cards.

### G-hook: Open on the surprise
stage: script
The first sentence states the most surprising concrete fact or stake about the place, in 15 words or fewer. Name the place in it. No greeting, no question to the viewer, and no opening that only says what the place is.

### G-structure: Hook, three beats, payoff
stage: script
After the hook, give three short beats that each add one new fact a viewer did not expect, in story order. End on a payoff line that answers or sharpens the hook. Each beat names something a photo or map can show.

### G-tone: A story told to a friend, not an encyclopedia
stage: script
Write like a curious person telling a friend: concrete nouns, active verbs, short sentences, dates only when they matter. No textbook phrasing, no filler, no hype words, and nothing the sources do not say.

### G-title: Name the place and the surprise
stage: title
The title names the place and the surprise, plainly and truthfully, in 70 characters or fewer. No clickbait, no all-caps words, no question marks.
